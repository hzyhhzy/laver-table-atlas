This proof is the first round of feedback. Further [Endpoint transport](<promoted-bms-feedback.md>) are supported for the second round, with $q(5)>D(D(D(h)+2)+2)$ provided; they are not directly repeated within the same $\tau$ window as this proof.

Under I3, fix

$$
R_h=(0^h)(1^h)(1^h),\qquad D(h)=D_2(R_h),
$$

The brackets indicate the columns of BMS, $h$ is the number of rows, $D_2$ is the parent table representing the total number of times new $[2]$ is taken to empty, that is, the number of times the original BM4 is taken to $[1]$. Let

$$
g(n)=n2^n,\qquad B=g^{258}(1),\qquad H_0=B+2.
$$

Combining the previous implementation and unified front insertion proofs, it can be obtained that

$$
\boxed{\quad
q(5)>D\bigl(D(h)+2\bigr)
\qquad(3\le h\le H_0).
\quad}
\tag{1}
$$

Here, $q(5)$ is the order parameter of the ordinary binary Laver table for the first occurrence of the period in the first row, which reaches 32. Therefore, (1) controls the order, not the number of elements. In particular, $h=11$, $h=18$, or $h=H_0$ can be taken. The number of rows on the outer layer is the result of a truly BMS calculation, $D(h)+2$, without the loss of taking logarithms.

This is a clear nested numerical lower bound, not an assertion that "the order of a finite row of BMSs exceeds the limit of the entire BMS." It does not complete the Hardy numerical comparison in the original IBLP $I[0][3][0]$, nor does it convert the fixed index decrease times into FGH.

## Existing conclusions used

The entire work is conducted within a fixed I3, embedded in a $\operatorname{Iter}(j)$ generated by $j$. $a(b)$ denotes application; ordinary compositions are denoted by $a\circ b$. $\gamma_0<\gamma_1<\cdots$ enumerates the critical points of this algebra, $\kappa_i=j^i(\gamma_0)$. Specifically, $\kappa_0=\gamma_0$, $\kappa_1=\gamma_1$, $\kappa_2=\gamma_2$, and $\kappa_3=\gamma_4$.

This proof utilizes three interfaces that have been previously presented in paper proofs:

1. [Implementation and deletion endpoint counting](<../iblp-laver-bms-20261013/ribbon-initialization.md>): Closed compatible groups of height $h\ge3$, seals $C=\gamma_r$, provide implementations for $R_h$, and have $D(h)<r$.
2. [Unify the front-end insertion](<../iblp-laver-bms-20261013/lined-front-insertion.md>): A $N$-lined $(\kappa_2,\theta_6,Z)$ chain, which provides a closed compatible group with a high $N+2$, and the closure is strictly below $Z$.
3. [Existing parameters have been inputted.](<../iblp-laver-bms-20261013/marked-prefix-rebase.md>): Without increasing the original $\theta_6$, construct a $B=g^{258}(1)$ segment of the $(\kappa_0,\kappa_1,x_B)$ chain, and also $x_B<\theta_6$. These are actual embeddings, not just the values of some recursive function.

The old [Three lines of feedback](<bms-height-feedback.md>) directly moved the chain to the root $\kappa_2$ using the same embedding, which required stronger additional endpoint conditions. Instead, it is now moved to the root $\theta_6$ first, and then the root is lowered to $\kappa_2$.

## Two feedback embeddings

Following the actual application terms of [Finite initialization](<../iblp-laver-bms-20261013/short-terminal-initialization.md>), let

$$
K=j_{(11)},\quad \mu=\theta_4,\quad b=K(j),\quad f=e(K).
$$

Points that are needed are like these:

$$
\begin{aligned}
b&:\kappa_2\mapsto\kappa_3\mapsto\mu\mapsto\theta_5,\\
f(\kappa_0)&=\kappa_3,\quad
f(\kappa_1)=\theta_5,\quad f(\theta_5)=\theta_6,\\
L_3&:\kappa_3\mapsto\theta_6\mapsto\theta_7,
\qquad \theta_7<\kappa_4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$

This set of $b,f,L_3$ are precisely the terms in the certificates, which identify two unknown embeddings based on the equivalence of finite point images. Definition

$$
E=L_3(K),\qquad P=L_3(f),\qquad
a=L_3(\mu),\qquad \tau=L_3(\theta_5).
\tag{3}
$$

Here, the new feedback embedding is denoted as $P$ to avoid confusion with Laver's critical point counting function $F$; in the verification procedure, its variable name is `F`. Based on the (2) and application point identity,

$$
\begin{gathered}
\theta_6<a<\tau<\theta_7<\kappa_4,\\
E(\kappa_0)=\kappa_2,\quad E(\kappa_1)=\theta_6,
\quad E(\theta_6)=\tau,\\
\boxed{P(\gamma_0)=\theta_6,\qquad
P(\gamma_1)=\tau,\qquad P(\tau)=\theta_7.}
\end{gathered}
\tag{4}
$$

For example, $\theta_6=L_3(\kappa_3)$, so

$$
E(\theta_6)=L_3(K)(L_3(\kappa_3))
=L_3(K(\kappa_3))=L_3(\theta_5)=\tau.
$$

Also because $\tau=L_3(\theta_5)$,

$$
P(\tau)=L_3(f)(L_3(\theta_5))
=L_3(f(\theta_5))=L_3(\theta_6)=\theta_7.
\tag{5}
$$

The formula (5) provides endpoint control for the entire interval: as long as $C<\tau$, there is $P(C)<\theta_7<\kappa_4$.

## A two-stage chain that reduces the root back to κ₂

Let $W=L_3(b)$. From (2),

$$
W:\kappa_2\longmapsto\theta_6\longmapsto a\longmapsto\tau.
$$

$W$ and $W(W)(W)$ have critical sequences respectively.

$$
\kappa_2\mapsto\theta_6\mapsto a,
\qquad \kappa_2\mapsto a\mapsto\tau.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{(\kappa_2,\theta_6,\tau)\text{ is 2-lined}.}
\tag{6}
$$

The critical point of $W(W)$ is $\theta_6$. It sends $\theta_6$ to $a$, sends $a$ to $\tau$, and fixes $\kappa_2$. Taking the application of $W$, we exactly obtain the two required sides.

If there is another $m$-lined $(\theta_6,\tau,Z)$, then [Lemma 2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06733v5#S2) provides

$$
(\kappa_2,\theta_6,Z)\text{ is }2\cdot2^m\text{-lined}.
\tag{7}
$$

The interface between the two chains is strictly consistent: the second point of the first chain is the common root of the second chain, and the end of the first chain is the starting point of the second chain. This is precisely the space that was not utilized in the original transport scheme.

## Restoring a sufficient length of chains from endpoint counting

Let the closed compatible group with height $h\ge3$ be sealed as $C=\gamma_r<\tau$. Write

$$
N=D(h),\qquad m=\lfloor\log_2N\rfloor.
$$

The implementation-based counting interface provides $N<r$, so

$$
\gamma_{2^m}\le\gamma_N<C.
\tag{8}
$$

From [Dougherty's Second Theorem 3.1(c)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9503204), each embedding

$$
j_{(2^{2^s}-1)}:\gamma_0\mapsto\gamma_{2^s}\mapsto\gamma_{2^{s+1}}
\quad(0\le s<m)
$$

Form a $(\gamma_0,\gamma_1,\gamma_{2^m})$ chain that constitutes a $m$-lined structure. Take the entire chain as a $P$-image and use (4), (8) to obtain

$$
(\theta_6,\tau,Z)\text{ is }m\text{-lined},
\qquad Z=P(\gamma_{2^m})<P(C)<\theta_7.
\tag{9}
$$

Merge (9) with two segments (6). Obtain the $(\kappa_2,\theta_6,Z)$ chain from the $2^{m+1}$-lined one using (7). Because

$$
N<2^{m+1},
$$

It can be shortened to exactly one segment containing $N$, while retaining the common root, starting point, and end point. Specifically, for adjacent segments $u:c\mapsto x\mapsto y$ and $v:c\mapsto y\mapsto z$, replace them with $v(v)(u):c\mapsto x\mapsto z$; this reduces the number of segments by one each time. This process can be carried out a finite number of times as specified.

The unified front insertion thus yields a closed compatible group with a high $N+2=D(h)+2$, which is strictly below $Z<\theta_7<\kappa_4$. Then, using the implementation with counting and $q(5)=F(4)+1$, we obtain

$$
\boxed{q(5)>D(D(h)+2).}
\tag{10}
$$

This proves a unified feedback lemma: the input must be a closed group that is sealed below $\tau$. Neither the decimal representation of $D(h)$ is required to be calculated, nor is it considered as a proven and usable linedness parameter.

## There are high-row numbers that satisfy the interval conditions.

The existing $B$-lined chain is $(\kappa_0,\kappa_1,x_B)$, where $x_B<\theta_6$. Take any $h-2$ segments before that, $3\le h\le B+2$, and denote the endpoints as $x_{h-2}$. After taking the $E$-image of the whole, we obtain

$$
(\kappa_2,\theta_6,E(x_{h-2}))
\text{ is }h-2\text{-lined},
$$

And

$$
E(x_{h-2})\le E(x_B)<E(\theta_6)=\tau.
\tag{11}
$$

The constructed height $h$ closed group seals $C_h$ satisfy the conditions introduced by the unified front end.

$$
C_h<E(x_{h-2})<\tau.
$$

Therefore, (10) applies to all $3\le h\le H_0$, and the proof is for (1). The eighteen lines can also use a separate sixteen-segment finite chain certificate (historical reference: `high_start_lining_certificate.py`), without the need for numerical expansion of $B$.

## A strict increase relative to the original lower bound

For each $n\ge1$,

$$
D(n)\ge n+3.
\tag{12}
$$

The proof is simple: Starting from $R_n$, we fix the expansion and retain the prefixes before the last column. Each non-zero last column expansion does not reduce the number of columns, and each zero last column deletion only reduces one column. Therefore, in the complete descent, we must first pass through two columns of prefixes $(0^n)(1^n)$, after which we have taken at least one step. The subsequent descent of these two columns exactly requires $n+2$ steps, including $n$ reductions in the height of the last columns and two zero column deletions.

Therefore

$$
D(D(h)+2)\ge D(h)+5>D(h).
\tag{13}
$$

Taking $h=H_0$ as the new lower bound, the new value strictly exceeds the previously obtained $D(H_0)$. This step only requires the prefix properties of the fundamental sequence and precise two-column reductions, without the unproven assumption that "the greater the general order, the longer the fixed index calculation".

## Verification scope and parts that are still unfinished

root_lowered_feedback_certificate.py (Historical reference: `root_lowered_feedback_certificate.py`) actually constructed eleven layers of closed groups, and verified each item individually:

- $C_{11}<\tau$; 
- $P(\gamma_0)=\theta_6$, $P(\gamma_1)=\tau$, $P(\tau)=\theta_7$; 
- The two testimonies of (6);
- A finite chain multiplication $2\cdot2^2=8$, with endpoint-preserving contraction to five segments.

This program was executed successfully, taking approximately 99 seconds. 476 names, 625 embeddings, and 3,302 point images were retained. There was a 180-second time limit for the execution, with an upper limit on the number of points, items, and facts. No enumeration of the target BMS was performed, and no large Laver tables were calculated. The process ended normally.

The program verifies finite algebraic interfaces. The deduction of any $N=D(h)$ is given by the paper argument of (8)—(11); it is not currently formalized in Lean.

This round has completed the numerical steps of "determining the number of rows for the next round using BMS calculations," but has not achieved the overall goal of the original IBLP 030. It should be noted that the feedback output only ensures that the closure is lower than $\theta_7$, not lower than $\tau$. The value (10) cannot be repeated indefinitely within the same budget; further iterations require re-proving the endpoint conditions. The next step is to establish a higher-level finite feedback or other quantitative simulations of the original IBLP, and to make a true numerical comparison with the growth functions selected under the original rules.
