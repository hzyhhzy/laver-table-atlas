## Notations and Basic Equations

All embeddings belong to the fixed $\operatorname{Iter}(j)$. $a(b)$ represents application, while $a\circ b$ represents ordinary composition. The following text describes a composite term $A=a_1\circ\cdots\circ a_t$, with the convention that $A(b)=a_1(a_2(\cdots(a_t(b))\cdots))$. It satisfies

$$
A(b(c))=A(b)(A(c)),\qquad A(b)(A(x))=A(b(x)).
\tag{2}
$$

These point image identities for LD and applications are based on word length induction; they do not derive from finite point image identities that involve two embeddings.

The notation $a\equiv_\delta b$ represents membership truncation equivalence. Usage:

$$
a(b)\equiv_{\operatorname{crit}(a)}b,
\qquad
a\equiv_\delta b\Longrightarrow
T(a)\equiv_{T(\delta)}T(b).
\tag{3}
$$

Truncation can reduce and take transitive closures. Only when the output**Strictly below** at a truncation point on one side can truncation equivalence be read as a equality of image points.

## Front-end insertion lemma

Let there exist a closed compatible group with a high $h$:

$$
U_0<\cdots<U_h<V_0<\cdots<V_{h+1}<C,
$$

$$
\operatorname{crit}(K_i)=U_0,\qquad
K_i(U_t)=V_t\ (t\le i),\qquad K_i(V_0)=V_{i+1},
\tag{5}
$$

There is a sealing embedding $L:V_0\mapsto V_{h+1}\mapsto C$, whose critical point is $V_0$. Let $U_0=\kappa_0,U_1=\kappa_1$.

Assume there is another embedding $D$ with point $\delta$ that satisfies

$$
\begin{gathered}
\operatorname{crit}(D)=\kappa_0,\quad
D(\kappa_0)=V_0,\quad D(\kappa_1)=\delta,\quad D(V_0)=V_1,\\
V_0<\delta<V_1,\qquad K_i\equiv_\delta D\quad(1\le i\le h).
\end{gathered}
\tag{6}
$$

Take any embedding $T$ of the critical point $\kappa_1$, and let it be $v=T(D)(\kappa_1)$. The new source, target, and owner are respectively taken as...

$$
\begin{aligned}
U'&=(\kappa_0,\kappa_1,T(U_1),\ldots,T(U_h)),\\
V'&=(T(V_0),v,T(V_1),\ldots,T(V_{h+1})),\\
K'&=(T(D),T(K_1),\ldots,T(K_h)).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$

The seal is taken as $T(L)$, with the end being $T(C)$.

Because $\kappa_0<\kappa_1<T(\kappa_1)$, by monotonicity and applying identity,

$$
T(V_0)<v<T(D)(T(\kappa_1))=T(\delta)<T(V_1).
\tag{8}
$$

After the truncation equation of (6) is applied, all old owners' images at the new source point $\kappa_1$ are of the form $v$; the strict guard of the read value is precisely $v<T(\delta)$. The remaining sides are directly transported by (2). The new lowest owner's image at the first target is $T(D)(T(V_0))=T(V_1)$, hence all (5) hold true. The sealed three points are also transported by (2).

Additionally, all new group owners are equal to the lowest owner at $T(\delta)$, while the new first positive target $v$ is strictly below this boundary. This common truncation is the inductive invariant required for the next insertion.

## Any segment of linedness provides a truncated identity

Let $\operatorname{crit}(Q)=\kappa_2$, define

$$
T=Q(j_{(10)}),\qquad Z=Q(j_{(3)}).
$$

Therefore

$$
\operatorname{crit}(T)=\kappa_1,\qquad T(\kappa_1)=Q(\kappa_2).
\tag{9}
$$

From $Q(j)\equiv_{\kappa_2}j$ and (4),

$$
Q(K)=T(Q(j))\equiv_{T(\kappa_2)}T(j),\qquad
Q(K)\equiv_{Q(\gamma_3)}Z.
\tag{10}
$$

First, name it as $T(j)(\kappa_1)$.

$$
T(j)(\kappa_1)<T(j)(T(\kappa_1))=T(\kappa_2)
$$

The first clause can be read legitimately. Therefore

$$
T(j)(\kappa_1)=Q(K)(\kappa_1)=Q(\kappa_3),
\quad Q(\gamma_3)<Q(\kappa_3)<T(\kappa_2).
\tag{11}
$$

After reducing the first cut-off boundary, it is merged with the second one to obtain

$$
T(j)\equiv_{Q(\gamma_3)}Q(j_{(3)}).
\tag{12}
$$

If $Q_*$ is an embedding of another critical point $\kappa_2$, or is an empty complex, then $Q_*(j)\equiv_{\kappa_2}j$. Therefore, for $X=T(Q_*(j))$, it is also the case that

$$
X\equiv_{Q(\gamma_3)}Z,\qquad X(\kappa_1)=Q(\kappa_3).
\tag{13}
$$

When $Q_*$ is an empty complex, simply take $X=T(j)$.

## Unifying the low owners required for the next insertion

Assume the current lowest possessor can be written as

$$
K_1=A(j_{(3)}),\qquad A=P\circ T\circ Q_*,
\tag{14}
$$

In this research paper, $T,Q,Q_*$ is as described in the previous section. $P$ is a compound term where the critical points of each factor are greater than $\kappa_1$. Therefore, $P$ fixes $\kappa_0,\kappa_1$. The first two sources in the current group are $\kappa_0,\kappa_1$, and all of their owners are equal to $K_1$ below some $\varepsilon>V_1$.

Put

$$
Y=P(X)=A(j),\quad Z'=P(Z),\quad
b=P(Q(\kappa_2))=A(\kappa_1),\quad
\zeta=P(Q(\gamma_3)),\quad W=A(j_{(2)}).
\tag{15}
$$

Given by application of identity

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\operatorname{crit}(Y)=\operatorname{crit}(Z')=\kappa_0,\\
&Y(\kappa_0)=Z'(\kappa_0)=b,\quad
Y(\kappa_1)=P(Q(\kappa_3)),\\
&Z'(\kappa_1)=\zeta,\quad
Z'(b)=P(Q(\kappa_3)),\quad
b<\zeta<Y(\kappa_1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
$$

Also, $\operatorname{crit}(W)=b>\kappa_1$, and
$W(b)=A(\kappa_2)=V_0$.

Definition

$$
D=W(Z'),\qquad \delta=W(\zeta).
\tag{17}
$$

Since $W$ is fixed by $\kappa_0,\kappa_1$, (16) gives)

$$
\operatorname{crit}(D)=\kappa_0,\quad
D(\kappa_0)=V_0,\quad D(\kappa_1)=\delta,
$$

$$
D(V_0)=W(P(Q(\kappa_3)))=W(Y)(\kappa_1)=K_1(\kappa_1)=V_1.
\tag{18}
$$

By transporting (16) strictly monotonically, we also obtain $V_0<\delta<V_1$. Transporting (13) twice results in

$$
K_1=W(Y)\equiv_\delta W(Z')=D.
$$

Due to $\delta<V_1<\varepsilon$, all current owners are equal to $D$ below $\delta$; therefore, all conditions of the forward insertion lemma are satisfied.

Insert the next $T'=Q'(j_{(10)})$, the new lowest possessor is

$$
T'(D)=A'(j_{(3)}),\qquad
A'=P'\circ T'\circ Q,
$$

$$
P'=T'(W)\circ T'(P).
\tag{19}
$$

Here, $T'(P)$ represents factor-by-factor transport. The critical points of each new factor are greater than $\kappa_1$: the critical points of the old factors and $W$ are greater than $\kappa_1$, and then we can take the $T'$-image. Therefore, the form of (14) is preserved, and the construction can continue indefinitely for a finite number of times.

## Induction starting from two layers and endpoint bounds

Take [Two-layer initialization](<short-terminal-initialization.md>)

$$
U=(\kappa_0,\kappa_1,\kappa_2),\quad
V=(\kappa_3,\theta_5,\alpha,\zeta_0),\quad
(K_1,K_2)=(f,J),\quad C_0=\rho<\theta_6.
$$

The $e$ here satisfies

$$
\operatorname{crit}(e)=\kappa_2,\quad
e(\kappa_2)=\kappa_3,\quad e(\kappa_3)=\theta_5.
$$

The first required low owners and cut-off points are taken

$$
D_0=e(j_{(3)}),\qquad \delta_0=e(\gamma_3).
\tag{20}
$$

It is given that (4),$D_0$ satisfy (6). Here, $f=e(K)\equiv_{\delta_0}D_0$, and
$J=p(f)\equiv_{\operatorname{crit}(p)}f$，
$\delta_0<\theta_5<\operatorname{crit}(p)$.

Given a sequence of lengths $m$ of linedness:

$$
\operatorname{crit}(Q_r)=\kappa_2,\quad
Q_r(\kappa_2)=a_r,\quad Q_r(a_r)=a_{r+1},
\quad 0\le r<m,
\tag{21}
$$

Among them, $a_0\ge\theta_6>\max(\mu,\rho)$. Insert $T_r=Q_r(j_{(10)})$ in sequence.

After the first insertion, the minimum owner is $T_0(e(j_{(3)}))$, which refers to the case where $P=T_0(e),T=T_0,Q_*$ is empty in (14). Therefore, all subsequent steps have been completed in the previous section. The height of inserting $r$ times is $r+2$.

If the current endpoint is $C_r<a_r$, due to (4) and (21),

$$
C_{r+1}=T_r(C_r)<T_r(a_r)
=Q_r(j_{(10)}(\kappa_2))=Q_r(\mu)
<Q_r(a_r)=a_{r+1}.
\tag{22}
$$

Starting from $C_0=\rho<a_0$, it follows that for every $r\le m$, there exists an $C_r<a_r$. This proves the initial theorem of unified transformation.

$$
\boxed{q(5)>D_2(R_{18}).}
