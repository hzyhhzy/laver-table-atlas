# Finite towers over the BMS limit realized by BLP · [中文版](01-blp-tower.zh-CN.md)

Work in ZFC + I3 with a single fixed $\operatorname{Iter}(j)$. The indices of the growth functions are literal terms and fundamental sequences of the original IBLP. We use the input $M=\mathcal Q(1)$ produced by the proof itself.

## 1. The bound and its natural input

Let $I$ be the initial term of the original IBLP and put

$$
A=I[0][1],\qquad R=I[0][3][0],\qquad
C=R[0][1]^8=I[0][2][1]^8.
\tag{1}
$$

Here $[1]^8$ means eight successive applications of the original $[1]$. The four rows of $A$ have the following cores and step sizes.

    01:1; 012:1; 0123:2; 234:1.

$C$ appends a fifth row $(0,1,5):1$. Neither pattern carries any marks. Define substitution at the first root by

$$
J(P)=A\mathbin{\frown}\varphi(P),\qquad
\varphi(0)=1,\quad\varphi(i)=4+i\ (i>0).
\tag{2}
$$

For every $n\ge2$ the original rules give the literal identity

$$
C[n]=J^{n-1}(A).
\tag{3}
$$

Thus $C[6]$ has 24 rows and $C[7]$ has 28 rows, as expressions of the original system.

To define the input, first introduce the fast BMS operators at finite types. At index zero, $K_M^{(r,u)}$ takes the base object $u$. A successor column iterates the lower-type object $2^x$ times. A limit column uses $M[2^x]$ of the original BM4. The last integer argument is always $x$. At $r=0$ the object is a numerical function. One type higher, iteration acts on the entire lower-type object. For the definitions, quantifiers over arguments, and tail comparison see [Typed comparison](proofs/scratch/iblp030-8h-20261015/hardy-root-typed-bms-bound.en.md) and [Tail locality](proofs/scratch/iblp030-8h-20261015/hardy-root-tail-locality.en.md).

Put

$$
f_0(x)=x2^x,\qquad B_h=(0^h)(1^h),
$$

$$
\mathsf S(u)(x)=K_{B_x}^{(0,u)}(x),\qquad
\mathsf D_r(u)(\cdots)(x)=K_{B_{x2^x}}^{(r,u)}(\cdots)(x)
\quad(1\le r\le6).
\tag{4}
$$

All applications below associate to the left.

$$
\mathsf T_*=\mathsf D_5(\mathsf D_4)(\mathsf D_3)(\mathsf D_2)(\mathsf D_1),
$$

$$
\mathsf W=\mathsf D_6(\mathsf D_5)(\mathsf D_4)(\mathsf D_3)(\mathsf D_2)(\mathsf D_1),
$$

$$
\mathsf U=\mathsf T_*(\mathsf T_*(\mathsf S)),\qquad
\mathcal Q=\mathsf U(\mathsf U(f_0)),\qquad
M=\mathcal Q(1),\qquad Y=\mathsf W(\mathsf S)(f_0)(1).
\tag{5}
$$

These finite recursive definitions are independent of $q(5)$. We prove

$$
\boxed{q(5)>2^Y>Y\ge H_{C[7]}(M).}
\tag{6}
$$

It follows that

$$
\boxed{q(5)>F_{C[6]}(M-1)+1.}
\tag{7}
$$

The input comes directly from the construction. The argument requires neither a preset decimal constant nor monotonicity in the input for general Hardy functions of the original system.

## 2. The original syntax and termination

In the finite class of expressions generated by the original safe class $D$, $A$, concatenation at a frozen zero anchor and $J$, we have

$$
J(0)=A,\qquad
J(Q)[n]=J(Q[n])\quad(Q\text{ is not a successor}),
$$

$$
J(Q+1)[n]=J(Q)^{\frown n}\quad(n\ge2).
\tag{8}
$$

The exceptional original $[1]$ gives $J(Q)\frown1$. The full-copy identity does not apply there. In the safe class both the full copy and the cut are always defined, and scanning triggers no completion. A copy in $J$ is anchored at the **first row** of the preceding block. Some other families use its last row.

All descendants stay in this class. The rank from a standard BMS cover, together with ranks for finite concatenation and an increasing index rank for $J$, makes the expansion tree well founded. This justifies the Hardy, FGH and type inductions below.

The four ingredients are [No completion in the safe class](proofs/output/iblp-01-equality-20260923/IBLP-01-no-completion-proof.md), [BMS upper cover of the safe class](proofs/output/iblp-01-equality-20260923/IBLP-01-BMS-upper-bound.md), [First-root substitution identity](proofs/scratch/iblp030-8h-20261015/hardy-root-continuation-interface.en.md) and [Closed syntax and a decreasing tree rank](proofs/scratch/iblp030-8h-20261015/hardy-root-first-root-tower.en.md).

## 3. Uniform bounds at finite types

Write $h=H_A$ and $\mathsf B_r(u)=F_A^{(r,u)}$ for the source operators using the original fundamental sequences and $x$ iterations at a successor. Exact substitution gives

$$
H_{C[7]}(x)=
\mathsf B_5(\mathsf B_4)(\mathsf B_3)(\mathsf B_2)(\mathsf B_1)(\mathsf B_0)(h)(x),
\quad x\ge2.
\tag{9}
$$

Every comparison quantifies over all base functions, lower-type arguments and final inputs. Write $\le_3$ for comparison at every final input of at least 3. The safe-class cover and the explicit descent paths for BMS seed heights give

$$
\mathsf B_0\le_3\mathsf S^2,\qquad
h\le_3\mathsf S^2(f_0),\qquad
\mathsf B_r\le_3\mathsf D_r\quad(r\ge1).
\tag{10}
$$

Here the matrix height of $\mathsf S$ is exactly $x$. The proof keeps this height throughout, without substituting $x2^x$. The first term is covered by $N(x)=2+x(x+1)/2$ and $x+2^x-1\ge N(x)+1$. For the higher types use $N(x)\le2^x$ and $x2^x-1\ge x+2$. The matrix paths use only the original indices 0, 1 and 2.

At every positive input and for every lower-type object, each $\mathsf D_r$ satisfies

$$
\mathsf D_r(u)\ge u^4.
\tag{11}
$$

To see this, reduce the corresponding BMS seed to two zero columns. Each zero column iterates the lower-type object at least twice. Repeated use of (11) along the chain of left-associated applications yields

$$
\mathsf W\ge\mathsf T_*^4,\qquad
\mathcal Q\ge_3 H_{C[7]},\qquad
\mathsf W(\mathsf S)\ge\mathsf U^4.
\tag{12}
$$

Each comparison first holds for whole function objects and is then evaluated at a particular argument. An inequality at one input would not suffice.

## 4. The construction supplies the stated input

The fast BMS operators are defined at small inputs as well. The original $B_1[1]$ consists of exactly two zero columns, so

$$
\mathsf S(f_0)(1)\ge f_0^{\circ256}(8)>3.
$$

From $\mathcal Q\ge\mathsf S(f_0)$ we obtain $M\ge3$. Using (12) and the strong prefix-growth property of the fast operators gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
Y
&\ge\mathsf U^4(f_0)(1)
\ge\mathsf U^3(f_0)(1)\\
&=\mathsf U(\mathcal Q)(1)
\ge\mathsf S(\mathcal Q)(1)
\ge\mathcal Q(\mathcal Q(1))\\
&=\mathcal Q(M)\ge H_{C[7]}(M).
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
$$

Only the last step invokes tail comparison, at the input $M\ge3$ actually produced. The construction does not evaluate the original source function at a smaller input.

## 5. Realizing the six levels by embeddings

We now relate (13) to $q(5)$. Sections 3–5 of [C7 composition proof](proofs/scratch/iblp030-8h-20261015/hardy-root-C7-q5-single-file.en.md) give the algebra. The endpoint conditions used here are as follows.

Take $K=j_{(11)}$ and the following application terms.

$$
b=K(j),\quad c=K(b),\quad d=c(K),\quad
e=d(j),\quad f=e(K),\quad A_*=c(d),\quad V=c(e),\quad Q=A_*(K).
$$

Put $\mu=K(\kappa_2)$, $\theta_5=K(\kappa_3)$, $a=K(\mu)$, $c_{\rm out}=K(\theta_5)$ and $\theta_8=j_{(9)}(\kappa_3)$. We have

$$
\kappa_3<\mu<\theta_5<a<c_{\rm out}<\theta_8<\kappa_4.
$$

The low-open fan is initialized with $K,j_{(3)}$, inserted at the front of each segment, all implicit last edges of the positive ports are retained, and both spare connections are preserved. After transmission, retain one spare belt, and move the ports of the two solid belts 1 downwards, resulting in a $\mathsf S$ contract with an actual height exactly $x$.

Put $T_*=j_{(10)}(j_{(3)})$ and $Z=A_*(T_*)$. The six higher-type environments are

| Level | Environment | Auxiliary entrance | Upper bound on the closed output |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | $V$ | $\kappa_3$ | $c_{\rm out}$ |
| 2 | $Q$ | $a$ | $A_*(\mu)$ |
| 3 | $Q\circ V$ | $c_{\rm out}$ | $A_*(\theta_5)$ |
| 4 | $Z$ | $A_*(\mu)$ | $A_*(T_*(\kappa_2))$ |
| 5 | $Z\circ V$ | $A_*(\theta_5)$ | $A_*(T_*(\kappa_3))$ |
| 6 | $Z\circ Q$ | $A_*(T_*(\kappa_2))$ | $\theta_8$ |

At each level the auxiliary entrance is exactly the preceding level's argument entrance, and the first payload point is its output. The owner of a successor copy therefore fixes all lower-domain coordinates while sending the current entrance to the previous whole object's output. The actors for nonzero copies have critical points above those coordinates. Each typed application thus has a realized entrance satisfying the required conditions.

The last layer only reads strictly open intervals. The complete proof is as follows:

$$
A_*(<a)<\theta_8,\qquad
(Z\circ Q)(<a)\le Z(Q(<a))<\theta_8,
\tag{14}
$$

Do not equate the ordinary compound with the supremum, nor claim that $A_*(a)<\kappa_4$.

Thus, by simply inputting the actual initial segment into $\mathsf W(\mathsf S)(f_0)$, we obtain an actual $Y$-line whose endpoints are below $\theta_8<\kappa_4$. $\mathsf U,\mathcal Q$ is only used for numerical comparisons and does not need to be inserted into geometric congruences. The common critical count is given as $q(5)>2^Y$, which, when combined with (13), results in (6).

Using known BMS well-orderings and practical replication trees for BMS compilation is necessary; there is no need to forge owner for the virtual endpoints of the oracle. [Natural Relative BMS Lemma, Section 4](proofs/scratch/iblp030-8h-20261015/lift-natural-relative-bms-oracle.en.md)

## 6. Hardy to standard successor convention FGH: Naturally sacrifices a layer

For this closed grammar class and any $x\ge2$, $y\ge x+1$, let $G^h$ use the base function $h=H_A$, and a recursive definition with a successor iteration of $y$ times, then

$$
G_Q^h(y)\ge F_Q(x)+1.
\tag{15}
$$

Proof by $Q$ that the well-foundedness follows from the inductive step, while quantifying all $x,y$. First, prove the monotonicity of the increment of $G_Q^h$ with respect to the prefix of the same input. In the subsequent steps, pair the two iterative tracks successively, maintaining a numerical difference of at least 1; the goal is to have at least $x+1$ iterations. In the limit step, use the precise prefix relationship $Q[x]\preceq Q[y]$, and then apply induction to the sub-items. This includes prefix exchanges with marks and frozen scanning, without assuming any ordinal subscript monotonicity.

By the first identity

$$
H_{C[7]}(M)=G_{C[6]}^h(M),
$$

In (15), taking $x=M-1,y=M$ gives (7). In the 2–4 section of [Complete item-by-item proof](proofs/scratch/iblp-I-to-I0-20261016/port-FGH-existing-bound-C6.en.md), this universal lemma is presented. This article provides a rework of its natural applications.

## The exact meaning of "several layers of BMS exponential tower" is marked as 7..

Definition

$$
\alpha_1=\Lambda,\qquad\alpha_{n+1}=\Lambda\cdot\omega^{\alpha_n}.
$$

The exact descending tree rank for the closed grammar is $\operatorname{rank}(C[n])=\alpha_n$. Therefore, (6), (7) are the Hardy bounds of $\alpha_7$ and the FGH bounds of $\alpha_6$; numerical conclusions are derived from the previous grammatical and geometric proofs.

If the symbol $\omega^\Lambda=\Lambda$ is used in addition, then $\alpha_2=\Lambda^2$, $\alpha_3=\Lambda^\Lambda$, $\alpha_6$ can be transformed into five right-hand combinations of $\Lambda$, and $\alpha_7$ into six. This is a shorthand representation with a clear closure premise; this article unconditionally uses $\alpha_n$ for recursion.

The descending tree rank of the entire $C$ is strictly greater than the smallest epsilon number for $\Lambda$. The results for finite layers have not been proven for $H_C(M)$ or $F_C(M)$; however, by the same cross-input lemma, $q(5)>F_{C[6]}(6)=F_C(6)$ is a valid small input inference. This epsilon number should not be automatically written as $\varepsilon_{\Lambda+1}$, unless it is proven that $\Lambda=\varepsilon_\Lambda$.
